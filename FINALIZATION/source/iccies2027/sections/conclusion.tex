\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We attached a canonical 668-term Vietnamese hate-speech lexicon to four
multilingual transformer backbones as an evidence channel and evaluated it on
the ViHSD test split. \texttt{sensitiveai-vi-custom} is retained as an
exploratory, post-hoc case study for fusion provenance, not a confirmatorily
selected primary model; the strongest standalone backbone is
\texttt{sensitiveai-vi-customdlr2stage} (validation macro-F1 0.7089, test
0.6638). On the focused backbone the canonical hybrid changes macro-F1 from
0.6575 to 0.6602---eight additional correct comments, paired-bootstrap
$p=0.29$---so we do not present it as an accuracy contribution.

The contribution is the provenance of the change. All 23 corrections are
rule-supported (16 \hate{} recoveries with median rule score 0.77, and 7
\clean{} de-flagging cases where the rule is correctly silent), and all 10
rule-driven regressions push towards \hate{}, localising the failure to an
aggressive channel rather than to coverage. A further 132 missed corrections
show the rule pointing right while a full-weight \clean{} channel blocks the fix.

The leak-free sensitivity run (671 entries) yields fusion macro-F1 0.6612 and
accuracy 0.8777 (23 corrections, 13 regressions); the standalone engine reaches
0.5328 and 0.8440. A preliminary three-seed check gives fusion-minus-AI
differences of $+0.0027$, $-0.0041$ and $-0.0010$ (mean $-0.0008$), so the
single-seed gain is not stable. The broader lesson is methodological: a hybrid
judged only by its aggregate metric looks null, whereas the same hybrid
decomposed by channel and direction yields an auditable procedure and a design
agenda. Future work: validate evidence-gated fusion, separate profanity from
political entries, restore negation and accent-less matching, and measure
whether the evidence record helps reviewers.
